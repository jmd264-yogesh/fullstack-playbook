# Business & Solution Foundations

**Level:** 🟢 Beginner - readable with no technical background

Everything else in this playbook - architecture, coding standards, testing, deployment - assumes you already know **what** you're building and **why**. This section is where that decision gets made.

Most engineering documentation starts at the technology. This section starts one step earlier, at the business problem, because that's where the most expensive mistakes actually happen. A beautifully engineered application that solves the wrong problem, or that solves a problem a five-line script would have solved, is still a failure - it just fails quietly, months later, when nobody uses it or nobody can explain what it's for.

## The core idea

::: tip Core idea
**Think in problems, not technologies.**
**Understand the complete delivery flow, not just your technical component.**
**Choose the simplest solution that creates the required business outcome.**
:::

An application, a script, an automation, an integration, a data platform, or an AI/ML model are all just *tools*. None of them is the goal. The goal is always a business outcome - time saved, errors removed, revenue enabled, a decision made faster or more reliably. Read [Vision & Principles](/vision-principles) for how this connects to the engineering principles the rest of the playbook is built on.

## Who this is for

This section is written to be readable by **everyone** - clients, business analysts, delivery managers, QA, data professionals, and engineers of every level - not just people who write code. If you only have ten minutes, read [What is a Business Problem?](/business-foundations/what-is-a-business-problem) and [Choosing the Right Solution](/business-foundations/choosing-the-right-solution). Together they explain the single most important habit this playbook is trying to teach: **understand the problem completely before you name a technology.**
