# Role Path: Senior Engineer / Tech Lead

**Level:** 🟡 Intermediate - assumes basic technical/business context

## Why you need this playbook

You're expected to make (and defend) decisions that juniors don't get asked to make: which architecture fits, when to take on technical debt deliberately, how to weigh a trade-off, and how to justify a choice to both engineers and non-technical stakeholders. This path leans into the trade-off and governance material, on top of everything a software engineer already needs.

## Reading list

1. [Choosing the Right Solution](/business-foundations/choosing-the-right-solution) - you'll be the one applying this framework under time pressure and defending the result.
2. [Application vs Data Platform](/business-foundations/application-vs-data-platform) - the single most common over-build you'll need to catch before it starts.
3. [Measuring Business Value](/business-foundations/measuring-business-value) - how to frame what your team ships in terms leadership and clients actually care about.
4. [Architecture Standards](/architecture/standards) and [API Standards](/architecture/api-standards) - the structural decisions you'll be accountable for.
5. [Solution Design](/delivery-lifecycle/design-phase) - your role in turning a chosen direction into a concrete, buildable design.
6. [Quality Gates Overview](/quality-gates/overview) and [Code Quality](/quality-gates/code-quality) - what you're expected to enforce in review.
7. [Governance Overview](/governance/overview) and [Roles & Responsibilities](/governance/roles-and-responsibilities) - where your decision authority starts and stops.
8. [Engineering Metrics](/kpis/engineering-metrics) and [Delivery Performance](/kpis/delivery-performance) - the numbers you'll be asked about.
9. [Incident Management](/operations/incident-management) - your role when something breaks in production.
10. [Case Studies Overview](/case-studies/overview) - real engagements, written up as short Challenge / Solution / Outcome summaries.
11. [Automation & Integration Overview](/automation-integration/overview) and [Data & Analytics Overview](/data-analytics/overview) - so you can steer a team away from over-engineering in either direction.

## You'll know you're ready when you can answer

::: info Ready check
- Can you explain, to a non-technical stakeholder, why you chose one architecture over another in terms of cost, risk, and time to value?
- What's your team's current technical debt, and what's the deliberate plan for it (not just "we'll get to it")?
- If a data-oriented teammate proposed a data platform for a problem, could you tell whether that's justified or overkill?
- What metrics would tell you, objectively, whether your team's delivery is healthy?
:::
