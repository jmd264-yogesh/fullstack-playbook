# Role Path: Client / Non-Technical

**Level:** 🟢 Beginner - readable with no technical background

## Why you need this playbook

You don't need to write code or choose a database. What you do need is enough understanding to ask good questions, sanity-check a proposal, and know why the team is spending time on things that aren't visible on screen - testing, security, monitoring. This path gives you that, in plain language, with no assumption you've ever built software before.

## Reading list

1. [What is a Business Problem?](/business-foundations/what-is-a-business-problem) - how a well-described problem looks, so you can tell if a proposal is actually solving yours.
2. [How Software Works in Real Life](/business-foundations/how-software-works-in-real-life) - one button click, traced through every system involved, in plain language.
3. [What is Full Stack?](/business-foundations/what-is-full-stack) - the four layers of a system, explained with a restaurant analogy.
4. [From Manual to Digital](/business-foundations/manual-to-digital) - why "build an app" isn't always the answer, and what the cheaper options look like.
5. [Application vs Data Platform](/business-foundations/application-vs-data-platform) - why having data doesn't automatically mean you need a data platform (a common and expensive over-build).
6. [Choosing the Right Solution](/business-foundations/choosing-the-right-solution) - the framework the team uses to justify what they're recommending to you.
7. [Measuring Business Value](/business-foundations/measuring-business-value) - how to know afterward whether the solution actually worked, not just whether it shipped.
8. [Vision & Principles](/vision-principles) - the standards the delivery team holds itself to, and why they exist.
9. [Delivery Lifecycle Overview](/delivery-lifecycle/overview) - the stages your request will move through, so you know what to expect and when.
10. [Case Studies Overview](/case-studies/overview) - real, anonymized examples of problems like yours being solved.

## You'll know you're ready when you can answer

::: info Ready check
- What is the actual business problem being solved - in one sentence, without naming a technology?
- Why did the team pick this kind of solution (application, automation, integration, data platform) instead of the others?
- What does "done" mean for this project, in terms of measurable outcomes, not just "it's live"?
- Why does testing, security, and ongoing monitoring cost time and money even after the visible feature is built?
:::
