## Scenario 1: Client Provides FRD

When the client provides a Functional Requirements Document (FRD) or a similar specification document, the first phase of the project focuses on **requirements validation, analysis, and planning**. The objective is to ensure that both business and technical teams have a shared understanding of the requirements before development begins.

## 1. Kickoff & Requirements Review

The Project Manager (PM) and Business Analyst (BA) conduct a kickoff meeting with the client's stakeholders to review the FRD and validate the requirements.

### Activities

* Review the scope, objectives, and business expectations.
* Clarify assumptions, constraints, and dependencies.
* Document open questions and action items.
* Conduct requirement walkthrough sessions if the FRD is extensive or complex.
* Ensure alignment between business and technical teams.

### Deliverables

* Requirement clarification log
* Meeting notes and action items
* Approved understanding of project scope

---

## 2. Requirement Breakdown & Estimation

Once the requirements are validated, the BA or Product Owner decomposes the FRD into actionable work items.

### Activities

* Convert requirements into user stories, epics, or development tasks.
* Define acceptance criteria for each requirement.
* Identify technical dependencies and integration points.
* Conduct effort estimation sessions with development teams.
* Review UI and UX requirements, wireframes, and design references.
* Prepare a high-level testing approach.

### Team Responsibilities

#### Business Analyst / Product Owner

* Create and prioritize backlog items.
* Define business rules and acceptance criteria.

#### Front-End & Back-End Developers

* Review technical feasibility.
* Estimate effort and identify dependencies.

#### UX Team

* Review existing designs.
* Create wireframes or design assets if required.

#### QA Team

* Draft a high-level test strategy.
* Identify test scenarios and validation points.

---

## 3. Key Deliverables

The following outputs should be produced before development begins:

### Product Backlog

* Prioritized user stories and tasks.
* Defined acceptance criteria for each item.

### Design Documentation

* Wireframes and mockups (if required).
* UI guidelines and design specifications.
* Style guides and reusable components.

### Testing Documentation

* High-level test strategy.
* Requirement-to-test-case mapping.

### Project Planning

* Development milestones.
* Sprint planning and release roadmap.
* Resource allocation and timelines.

### Documentation Control

* Version-controlled FRD.
* Baseline requirements document.
* Traceability between requirements and implementation.

---

## 4. Acceptance Criteria Definition

Acceptance criteria define the specific conditions that must be met for a feature or requirement to be considered complete.

### Example

**Requirement:**

> New user registration sends a welcome email.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

* User successfully registers using a valid email address.
* Welcome email is generated automatically.
* Email is delivered within 5 minutes of registration.
* Email content matches the approved template.
* Email contains the correct user-specific information.

### Purpose

* Provides a clear definition of success.
* Eliminates ambiguity.
* Forms the basis for QA test cases.
* Ensures alignment between business expectations and implementation.

---

## 5. Change Control Process

Because the FRD typically represents the agreed project scope, all changes must follow a structured change management process.

### Activities

* Track FRD versions and revisions.
* Document all requested changes.
* Assess impact on scope, timeline, cost, and resources.
* Obtain stakeholder approval before implementation.
* Maintain a change request register.

### Benefits

* Prevents uncontrolled scope expansion.
* Maintains project transparency.
* Ensures stakeholder accountability.

---

## 6. Artifacts & Handoffs

After requirements are finalized and approved, work is handed off to implementation teams.

### UX Team Deliverables

* Design assets
* Icons and imagery
* Style guides
* Annotated wireframes
* Design specifications

### Development Team Deliverables

* Feature branch setup
* Technical implementation
* Code reviews
* Unit testing

### QA Team Deliverables

* Test cases
* Test execution reports
* Defect tracking
* Validation against acceptance criteria

### Definition of Done

A feature is considered complete when:

* All acceptance criteria are met.
* Code review has been completed.
* Unit and functional testing have passed.
* No critical defects remain open.
* Business validation has been completed.

---

## 7. Risks & Mitigation

### Risk: Ambiguous Requirements

Poorly defined requirements can lead to multiple interpretations and significant rework.

**Example:**

> The system shall respond quickly.

The term "quickly" is subjective and lacks measurable criteria.

#### Mitigation

* Conduct structured requirement review sessions.
* Use the "Three Amigos" approach:

  * Business Analyst
  * Developer
  * Tester
* Define measurable acceptance criteria.
* Review all requirements collaboratively before development begins.

---

### Risk: Missing Functional Scenarios

Requirements documents may omit:

* Error handling scenarios
* Validation rules
* Edge cases
* Exception workflows

#### Mitigation

* Identify and document corner cases during review sessions.
* Ask scenario-based questions such as:

  * What happens if a required field is left blank?
  * What happens if a service is unavailable?
  * What happens when invalid data is submitted?
* Use prototypes, wireframes, and use cases to validate expected behavior.

---

## Summary

When an FRD is provided by the client, the project team's primary objective is to validate, refine, and operationalize the requirements. Through structured reviews, backlog creation, acceptance criteria definition, testing preparation, and change control processes, the team ensures that development begins with a clear, agreed-upon understanding of the project scope and expected outcomes.
